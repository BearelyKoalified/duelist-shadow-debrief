Developing the Duelist and Shadow

Designers James Woodmansey (Shadow lead) and Kevin Readen (Duelist lead) explain how the two classes coming with PoE2's full release were built, what got cut, and why. Q&A hosted by David Lane. Watch the talk

The short version

  • Shadow is built around stealth you can enter at any time. Shroud, the first skill you get, has no conditions and its cooldown resets on kill.
  • Stealth has no speed penalty. You run, dodge roll and sprint at full speed. Each monster shows a detection cone, and you are spotted faster the closer you are.
  • Traps arrive as a weapon type. They cannot trigger while you are stealthed, so you stack several and they all fire the moment you break stealth.
  • Bosses see the whole arena unless blinded. Boss stealth comes in windows of a few seconds, with a blind-immunity gap between them.
  • Duelist brings swords and "ready stance". Hold a skill to enter a stance that upgrades your next skill. Blood stance and sand stance are stronger versions.
  • Neither class is "true melee". Duelist gets medium-range skills tied to the sword. Shadow closes distance with dashes on every dagger attack.
  • Still to come: Templar (aimed at a battle mage) and Marauder (rage, berserking, thrown axes).

The stealth loop

1 · ShroudEnter stealth instantly. Blinds enemies and leaves a shade.
2 · Set upThrow traps (they wait), move at full speed, stay out of detection cones.
3 · StrikeDagger attack dashes in. Shades copy it. Darkness adds damage.
4 · DetonateStealth breaks and every stacked trap fires in the order thrown.
5 · ResetA kill resets Shroud's cooldown.

↺ Wipe the pack and you loop back to step 1. Fail and Shroud stays on cooldown, which is the punishment.

Breaks stealth

  • Using or triggering a skill
  • Hitting an enemy, or being hit
  • Your minions hitting an enemy
  • Touching a monster's detection cone

Does not break stealth

  • Throwing traps
  • "Ethereal" chaos skills such as Ethereal Knives
  • Hits from skills you used before entering stealth
  • Damage in the first half second or so (grace period)
How stealth was designed 2:32
  • Stealth, daggers and traps were the three things always planned for Shadow. Stealth was on the design doc before James joined seven years ago.
  • Classic stealth-game rules were ruled out: no crouch-walking, no patrol routes, no per-skill noise or monster intelligence levels. Stealth is one part of the game, and the magic theme justifies simple rules.
  • Sight cones and a player-centred detection circle were tried. The final version is a visible range around each monster.
  • The cone started as a timer display. While nerfing stealth, the team made touching the cone the thing that detects you.
  • Darkening the whole screen made night areas unreadable, so the screen is brightened inside detection ranges with a brighter ring on the edge.
Why Shroud has no conditions 6:00
  • Early versions gated stealth behind distance from monsters or a long cooldown. Conditional stealth was confusing and cooldown gating was frustrating.
  • Now you can always enter stealth. If you get detected, the reason is clear, and larger detection ranges teach you not to Shroud into the middle of a pack.
  • The cooldown resets on kill so hit-and-run works, but you cannot spam it on bosses.
  • Shroud is the first skill so no player can miss the class's core mechanic. Its working name was Smoke Bomb.
  • James named removing the cooldown and conditions as the moment Shadow became fun.
Blind and boss fights 7:40
  • Blinded enemies have a smaller detection range. Because players can shrink ranges, base ranges can be set higher, which keeps permanent stealth in check.
  • Any boss with an arena sees you everywhere in it unless it is blinded. You make your own short windows by blinding it.
  • If an enemy kills a shade it loses blind.
  • Bosses do not stand idle while you are stealthed. They lash out, and being hit breaks stealth.
  • From the Q&A: bosses also get a window of blind immunity, so a party cannot keep one member stealthed forever.
Five problems stealth caused, and the fixes 9:10
ProblemFix
Melee rangeYou cannot walk up to enemies unseen, so every dagger attack dashes from stealth, including the basic attack.
Delayed hitsEach skill's use time is tracked. Only skills used after entering stealth can break it. A half-second grace period covers the rest.
Safe killsSkills that kill without breaking stealth would win the game, so those skills are "ethereal" chaos damage that enemies do not notice.
CooldownsWaiting in stealth for cooldowns was dull, so no cooldown recovers while stealthed.
WanderingIdle monsters wandered too much and ruined ambushes. Wandering was cut back and reused for a "high alert" search state.

Can you just run past everything? Yes, but there is no hard block. Detection ranges are high, detours are long, and you give up drops and XP for a little safety.

Traps

  • A weapon type, not a modifier. Unlike PoE1 you cannot mix trap behaviour onto other skills. Traps deal normal attack damage.
  • Fast to throw, limited by cooldowns and a placement cap shared across all trap skills.
  • Held while stealthed. They fire together when stealth breaks, which gives the "alpha strike" of five or six skills landing at once.
  • Entering stealth refunds one cooldown use on every trap, since cooldowns do not recover in stealth.
More trap details 14:00
  • Shrapnel Trap stands in for a basic attack. It is physical and applies impale.
  • A trap exploding is now a real triggered skill, which removes the old double meaning of "trigger".
  • Trigger radius and arming time are generous by default so traps feel good without investment.
  • Cooldowns push you to carry several trap types, and the team mapped combos between them in many orders.
  • When stealth breaks, every trap fires even if the first one kills the target. Leaving the rest unfired felt like a bug.
  • You can ignore stealth and throw traps like grenades, or stay stealthed and detonate them yourself for bonus damage and area.

Shades, darkness and impale

Shades

  • Decoy and shadow clone in one. They do not attack alone.
  • They copy your melee attacks made from stealth, at a damage penalty.
  • The damage counts as yours, so all your stats apply.

Darkness

  • A resource you build, then spend on stealth attacks for bonus damage.
  • Replaced a flat stealth damage bonus that was too strong and gave nothing to invest in.
  • Shares the rage bar, filling from the opposite end.

More on shades 17:56
  • Enemies run at a shade to attack it. That is a deliberate nerf to stealth, because they are likely to spot you on the way.
  • Shades reuse the Duelist work that turned Ancestral Call style clones into an extension of the player. The team hopes to extend it to Mirage Archer and similar effects.
  • Targeting was hard. Shades now attack your target by default so the alpha strike lands. A support gem to spread them out is undecided.
  • When a shade has no target it either swings at air or does nothing, chosen by situation.
  • Dashing through walls was wanted, but pathfinding does not support it yet.
  • Wording the tooltip was a headache: the shade neither "uses" nor "copies" the skill. It is part of your attack.
More on darkness 21:37
  • Main source: the Engulfed in Darkness debuff. Enemies explode and drop a darkness remnant on death, or when immobilised as you engulf them.
  • Engulf also blinds, giving the class blind without using Shroud.
  • Grim Bargain fuels the large "eclipse" moments in the trailer.
  • Sharing the rage bar saves a UI element and gives rage real competition on stealth builds.

Ideas that changed or were cut

Evolved became something that ships Shelved may return Cut dropped
  • EvolvedConfusing monsters into fighting each other. Monster damage barely scratches other monsters. The idea became stealing enemy shadows, then Nightmare: your shades turn into invincible tentacled fiends that scale like totems.
  • EvolvedPhysical dagger spells. They needed attack scaling and attack restrictions, so they became attacks. A hybrid battle mage is saved for later.
  • ShelvedTeleporting between shades. You cannot control where shades stand, slow animation fought snappy gameplay, and it filled no gap. "Knifed for now."
  • ShelvedTrap meta skill. Traps are weapons now, so socketing other skills into one is unclear. Possibly after release.
  • ShelvedMines. Separate base types meant half your drops were useless. Stealth traps plus the detonate skill already cover the playstyle.
  • CutPower charge interactions. In PoE1 that link came from crit. The Monk already uses power charges on the same attribute.
  • CutBackstab damage by direction. Tried, but you cannot control which way a monster faces.

The blood combo

1 · ParryOff-hand sword parries and marks the enemy as parried.
2 · Bloodied BladeHitting a parried enemy explodes and applies bleed.
3 · Blood stanceHolding the skill enters the upgraded stance. Basic slashes now bleed.
4 · HemorrhageCompresses the bleed so its damage lands quickly. Blood loss stays.
5 · Rending DashDash through to cash in the remaining blood loss.

Skip the parry: spending a frenzy charge enters blood or sand stance without it.

What Kevin set out to build

Fixed constraints

  • Swords must feel melee, slashing and piercing
  • Strength and dexterity class, sharing a tree start with Mercenary
  • Cyclone must feel good. It was the first thing he wrote down.

His own goals

  • Limited elemental presence
  • Flow from one skill into the next
  • Grounded movements a person could plausibly make
  • Mechanics that work outside sword skills
Design philosophy 33:22
  • A 1.0 release without swords was not an option.
  • Meet player expectations first, then exceed them. The left stick moves you, Escape opens the menu, and a sword should feel like a melee weapon.
  • Complex features are easy to design. Simple ones that feel good are hard.
  • Planning began with a pass over every PoE1 skill usable with a sword, marking each as boring, questionable or worth keeping.
  • Classes are designed "from the sand up", starting with the first skill, because exiles start on a beach with nothing.

Keeping it readable 48:12
  • Bloodied Blade alone touches parry, bleed, frenzy charges, ready stance and blood stance. Kevin's claim is that it plays more smoothly than it reads.
  • An early Perforate had five stat tabs running off the screen. That was judged unacceptable.
  • Stances are spent on existing resources such as frenzy charges. Sword-only charges were rejected so skills stay part of the wider game.
  • Skill icons change while you are in ready stance to show which skills will benefit.
  • Keywords should mean the same thing everywhere they appear.
How the team iterates 52:53
  • Get the first act playable as early as possible to learn whether the class is fun.
  • Lean into what works. When something fails, pivot while reusing as much finished work as possible.
  • "Is it cool?" counts for a lot, even though it never shows up in a spreadsheet.
  • Ready stance drew on Guild Wars assassin combos (starter, middle, finisher).
  • Feedback comes from playtests and threads, inside and outside the studio.

The melee question

  • "Just hit things with swords" echoed around the office for the whole project. Kevin and Jonathan spent hours on what that means.
  • Kevin's position: standing right in front of an enemy is not what suits PoE2 or most action RPGs. A class must work on the beach with no gear and later against a god.
  • The compromise: short to medium range effects that clearly come from the sword, plus movement built into Rending Dash, Cyclone and both stance skills.
  • Variety limit: swinging right-to-left instead of left-to-right is not a new skill. At one point he was told Duelist had hit its "spin limit".
Supporting systems 58:49
  • Ascendancies were not detailed, beyond a look at the Knight in the keynote.
  • The passive tree gains new stats, clusters and paths.
  • New support gems arrive, with a push for the recommended ones to be good.
  • Duelist work led to wider changes to bleed and support gems.

Ideas that changed or were cut

Saved nearly cut, shipped Evolved became something that ships Shelved may return
  • SavedWorld Cutter. Began as "Big Sword Slam". Slams belong to other weapons, so it was nearly killed. Kevin kept working on it quietly until it was good.
  • SavedReady stance input. Holding one button while pressing another met internal pushback. Kevin had to fight for it.
  • EvolvedReal martial stances. European fencing and other styles would have needed huge animation work. The idea shrank into ready stance.
  • EvolvedImpale on swords. Moved to daggers, which Kevin thinks is the better home.
  • ShelvedA physical shield throw. Kevin wanted a real shield, not a spectral copy. That raised questions about losing stats and shield skills mid-flight. Timers, bounce counts and wall bounces all felt wrong up close.

Audience questions 1:03:58

  • How long does a class take?Usually 4 to 12 months of active development. Rough plans for every class date back to the start of PoE2. Duelist began shortly after Druid and stalled for weeks while Kevin was moved to other skills.
  • Is stealth dagger-only?Shroud is. Later sources of stealth do not all need daggers, and you can keep a dagger on your second weapon set. James wants Snipe to work well from stealth.
  • Why lightning on swords and not fire?Avoiding elements entirely proved too hard. Lightning fit "fast" best. A frozen sword felt brittle. An oil-coated fire sword is possible later.
  • How does melee keep up with ranged?Mobility. Ranged characters slow down while attacking. Shadow dashes everywhere and Duelist skills have movement built in.
  • Does the Knight's squire count as a minion?Yes. It is a companion you can scale as a minion, and it also works as support for a normal sword build.
  • How do you track every interaction?Mostly by keeping it in their heads, backed by colleagues who catch what they miss.
  • Will parry hold up in endgame?Parry duration and range are improving, Duelist adds an active way to apply it, and frenzy charges let you skip it. Less interactive builds are fine too.
  • Does stealth work on endgame bosses?Yes, no boss is immune. You blind the boss, get 2 to 3 seconds to set up, then need something else to do until blind immunity ends.
  • Was Count Geonor an inspiration?Yes. The team reviewed every sword-using monster for movement ideas. A unique with twin ice walls and a sword slam grew alongside World Cutter and shares its animation.
  • What makes dual wielding worth it?Some penalties were reduced. Dual swords grant parry. Many skills land more hits in a single attack: Shadow Extraction hits 3 times instead of 2, so 50% more shades.
